Contributor guide#

Why the code is as it is, module by module, for anyone changing it, human or agent. Each section lists what must stay true and the measurement behind it. New behaviour goes behind a config key unless there is evidence to make it a default.

Repository#

  • Keep saved objectives, physical bounds and historical outputs intact. Never edit a source checkout or data directory merely referenced by a run manifest.

  • Report objective call counts separately from wall time.

  • Push or publish only when the user has authorised it. Verify the remote and target branch first, and do not force-push over others’ work.

  • What is specific to the Grendel cluster (install, sync, Slurm headers, run definitions) is in the private repository AarhusCosmology/ClientCluster. hpc/ here holds the generic tools.

Before changing dataset/augment.py#

client.py augment adds a profile study’s true evaluations to a run’s training set through the acquisition’s density filter; read docs/augment_training.md. Keep these:

  • Both densities in the filter come from the same k-NN estimator and whitening (the training set’s density at the candidate, and the law read off the training set at the candidate’s true log L). An analytic target density on one side reintroduces the estimator’s high-dimensional bias.

  • One pass with retention [1 − ρ/ρ(L)]₊. Drawing until no candidate is short admits nearly every candidate that k-NN noise makes look short: when that was tried it took 44% of the candidates of a training set already at its target. tests/test_augment.py guards this.

  • It writes RUN/augmentation/ and changes no existing file. Coordinates are compared at single precision, the training set’s.

  • The first training with the points uses training.augment_schedule_factor; the marker augment_it_N.trained.json is what makes it happen once, also across restarts.

  • A lower msre c and a cold start were both measured as alternatives to the longer schedule and rejected (table in the doc). Do not swap them in without that measurement.

Before changing dataset/peak_samples.py#

The injected points (prior.n_inject, acquisition.n_inject) and their window (INJECT_WINDOW, 16); read docs/injection_window.md. Keep these:

  • gaussian_peak_samples(..., window=None) fits every point; the loop always passes the window.

  • _fit_quadratic is shared with the profile code (profiles/, dataset/profile_augmentation.py). The window selects points before the fit and caps widths after it; the fit itself is not touched.

  • On a real likelihood in a wide box the fit to every point is not a poor approximation, it is meaningless (residual of tens of thousands of log-units). Read the log line: a “vertex value” far above the best training value, or a “fit rms” of thousands, says the injected points are not near the peak.

  • The window is in units of T n / 2 below the best, with a floor of three points per coefficient. Narrower windows and small fixed counts were erratic on the non-Gaussian likelihood; a hold-out choice of the window and drawing in rounds were tried and not adopted.

  • The document has two kinds of depths: true values (a pair of cluster runs, and the runs’ own old injections) and the runs’ last networks standing in for the truth (everything else). Do not quote the second kind as true values.

Before changing dataset/boost.py or sampling/modes.py#

acquisition.boost replaces an iteration’s tempered batch by posterior samples of the network, the same number from every mode; read docs/boost.md. Keep these:

  • Opt-in. Without the key the loop is exactly as it was, under MPI too: the one extra bcast_idle (the workers sleep while the master samples, and learn whether it found a mode to boost) is sent only in a boost iteration.

  • A boost iteration’s true calls are the n asked for: no tempered batch, no injection. The one exception is a network without a mode (sampling.modes.NoModeFound): that iteration is an ordinary one on every rank (client.draw_boost_points, which also tells the workers; tests/mpi_boost_fallback_smoke.py), with its tempered batch and injection and a record in RUN/boost/fallback_it_N.json. Catch that error only: another failure of a boost must still stop the run. By default points bypass the density filter. Opt-in density_filter: true uses a balanced T=1 density target and may underfill; see docs/posterior_density_boost.md. Filtered rows must never receive the unweighted evidence correction.

  • For the default unfiltered boost, the same number from every mode, whatever the weights. Each mode’s mean of exp(true minus network) must be known equally well, and the mode the network under-rates is the one a proportional split would starve.

  • Modes are groups of maxima, not of T = 1 walkers. A walker sits ndim / 2 log-units under its peak, which hides a barrier of that size, and a walker stranded on the barrier joins the two sides (the first version did both on SINU). Climbs that stall more than barrier under a maximum they are joined to are left out for the same reason.

  • Mode weights come from the per-mode evidences (bridge sampling), never from the tempered chain’s split between modes: that was off by 0.2 in log-odds per network, measured. An evidence that is not finite stops the boost before any true call (mode_log_evidence): the weights are fractions of one sum, so it would leave none of them known.

  • The bridge’s mixture is fitted to the walkers of one half of the chain and bridged with the other half, a new halving at every repeat (bridge_log_evidence(..., walkers=); both callers, posterior_modes and sampling/mixture.py, pass their chain’s walkers). Never random rows of one chain: a mixture lies higher at the points it was fitted to, and where the autocorrelation time spans many stored rows, random rows share their points. That made ln Z 0.26 to 0.27 low on two 44-parameter networks, with a repeat spread of 0.01 that hid it (docs/boost.md, “Weights”; tests/test_bridge_split.py). The repeat spread means something only with halves that do not depend on each other.

  • The corrected weights are only as good as the effective number of points behind them. Keep that number in the log and the report. A point the likelihood refused (a -inf return, or the run’s refused value) has weight zero and stays in the denominator of its mode’s mean, as in the true check (hpc/analysis/true_check.py), and is counted (zero_density, refused); the two programs must keep the same rule. A point without any value (NaN, +inf) still withholds the corrected weights of every mode (boost_report, correction_withheld_reason, failed): do not give it weight zero, its mode’s mean is not known. Never drop refused points from the denominator: that over-rates the mode they were drawn in.

  • mode_depth (opt-in) leaves out a mode whose highest maximum lies more than that below the highest mode’s, before its chain is run; the highest mode always stays. It is decided by the modes’ maxima, never by their weights (the same-number rule above is about weights the network may get wrong; a mode hundreds of log-units down is not one of those). Without the key the modes and the search’s info are as before. auto (posterior_modes resolves it) is temperature * ndim / 2 with the tempered chain’s temperature; the info’s mode_depth holds the number.

  • balance_floor (opt-in) gives a mode lighter than the floor its equal share times its weight over the floor (dataset/boost.py mode_shares; the filtered path cuts the clouds in select_posterior_boost). Without the key draw_boost and select_posterior_boost are the equal shares (tests/test_boost.py, tests/test_posterior_boost.py): keep that. It is for small groups of walkers that min_share 0.005 lets through, which the posterior pilot cannot check; a mode at or above the floor keeps its equal share.

  • Two recommended boost settings (docs/boost.md, “Two recommended settings”): near-Gaussian (the reference runs’ block) and known non-Gaussian (the axion pilot branch’s). Only documentation: no key selects either, and no default changed. The measured split is the training domain: it cost the 49-parameter and HiLLiK branches accuracy (tails too high at the first domain network; 2.5x and 6.5x the reference’s error at the last boosts) and is what the axion run needed. Do not make either set a default without the confirmation runs’ full results. What decides it is mostly the output scale the far points set (“What the training domain changes”: retrainings of five runs’ first boost networks); a choice of the three candidates by their held-out error near the best picked the better one in all five, but it is not built (one seed each).

  • A true value that is not finite is not trained on (the loop’s own filter) and is counted in the report.

  • RUN/boost/boost_it_N.csv is a record; the points themselves are in training set N+1 (run/layout.py).

  • finish_boost matches each evaluated point to a drawn one at single precision. The launcher rounds a batch to single precision and keeps it inside the box as single precision holds it (hpc/launch.py, evaluate), so a point drawn in the last digit before an edge comes back one step inside. Such a point is the nearest unmatched drawn one within MOVED_BY_A_LAUNCHER units in the last place and is recorded where it was evaluated; anything farther still stops the run. Matched bit for bit it stopped every run under the launcher whose boost drew one: rare on a good network, certain on one pressed against its box. The unit suite and the MPI smokes run client.py directly and did not see it. After a change to this match, to the launcher’s rounding, or to anything else that compares evaluated coordinates with chosen ones, run a small analytic case through hpc/launch.py to its boosts.

  • The pooled error of a boost (error in its report, the two log lines after the modes) is a measurement of the network at points it had not seen, printed for every boost, filtered or not. It decides nothing: the number of boosts is the user’s, and the loop’s convergence test must not be applied to boost iterations. It is not the evidence correction, which stays withheld for filtered rows.

Before changing run/combine.py (domain splitting)#

python client.py combine puts runs of one objective on adjacent boxes in one parameter into one emulator; read docs/combine.md. Keep these:

  • The combined network is one flat Keras graph: every part’s layers copied under part<k>_ with the same weights (rebuild). The profile code copies a model with clone_model layer by layer at double precision (copy_model_dtype); a nested sub-model is not what it was tested on.

  • The blend is (1 - s) A + s B with two SmoothStep weights (model/network.py, registered so that load_model finds them), each exactly 0 or 1 outside the band: there the value is exactly one network’s. A + s (B - A) rounded it by up to 0.12 in float32 next to a wall. The smootherstep’s first and second derivatives vanish at the band’s ends (the profiles’ curvature).

  • Each part’s network is used inside its own region only (RegionWall in the graph) unless --no-walls: a network has maxima beyond its region, and another part’s region can reach into its range.

  • The parts must record the same objective: run.identity.differences with only the split parameter’s bounds removed. Do not widen that: a narrower Cobaya prior on the split parameter changes the objective by a constant, and the networks would no longer share one scale.

  • The combined region is every part’s cells re-expressed in the combined box’s units (rebox: distances unchanged, tested to 1e-12). A combined emulator is sampled cell by cell (sampling/mixture.py), each cell weighted by its own evidence. Identical normalized ellipsoids are merged only in this outer union, with source-cell provenance; individual network walls stay intact. Mixed region/no-region inputs are rejected. Consume verified immutable copies of all inputs, record their hashes, and publish the complete output by directory rename; never expose a partial run without its combine.json marker.

  • The training points are the parts’ points with a value; refused ones are left out, since the parts’ refused values differ.

  • The loop refuses a directory with combine.json (client.py main): it is not a run to continue.

  • tests/test_combine.py covers the values in and around the bands, the walls, the files, the consumers (load_snapshot, restrict_model), the refusals and the loop’s refusal.

Before changing run/export.py#

python client.py export writes a network as a TensorFlow SavedModel that loads without CLiENT; read docs/export.md. Keep these:

  • The default export is the network inside the region of its boosts (restrict_model), as CLiENT’s own consumers use it; --no-region is the bare network. Do not make the bare network the default: a boost network has false maxima outside its region.

  • No export is accepted until it has been reloaded in a process that cannot import CLiENT (python -I, another working directory) and compared with the source. The tolerance is absolute plus relative to the value: traced and eager TensorFlow order operations differently, and at walls of 1e5 and more they differ by a few float32 steps.

  • The network comes from load_snapshot, so that the hashes in emulator.json are of the bytes exported, and OUTPUT is built beside itself and renamed last, so that a refused or failed export leaves nothing.

  • A relative OUTPUT must work: the reload runs in the staging directory, so paths are resolved first (client.py export RUN exported failed while every test used absolute ones).

  • A loaded SavedModel must be kept in a variable while it is called; a one-expression load-and-call frees its variables (the test’s first version failed that way).

Before changing the convergence of the ordinary loop#

convergence.metric: tempered_batch_scatter (convergence/convergence.py::tempered_verdict, client.py::tempered_convergence); read docs/tempered_convergence.md. Keep these:

  • Opt-in. With gaussian_posterior_drift the loop, its broadcasts and its files are as they were. With the new metric the hand-over travels in the broadcast that already carried the stopping decision, and one more broadcast at the start tells the workers a recorded start.

  • The threshold is in units of the sampling temperature, on the scatter (not the rms), for two networks running. 1.2 is measured (an analytic suite at four temperatures, and a real 49-parameter run branched at the network where it is met). Do not change the default, the measure or the unit without repeating that.

  • It belongs to the ordinary loop alone. Once the boosts have begun it is not evaluated: a boost reports its error at posterior points against true values and the user decides when to stop (-i, max_iterations, acquisition.boost.boosts). Do not add an automatic stop to the boost phase.

  • With -i nothing follows from the verdict: it is printed and recorded only.

  • A hand-over is recorded in RUN/boost/start.json, and every later use of the boosts’ start (the background, the region, the acquisition, a continued run, the report in hpc/) reads the earlier of that and acquisition.boost.start_iteration. Never rewrite the run’s copy of its settings.

Before changing the mixing test of the modes#

acquisition.boost.mixing and python client.py modes (sampling/modes.py, sampling/detect.py); read docs/boost_modes_mixing.md. Keep these:

  • Opt-in. With mixing absent posterior_modes takes the fixed-barrier path unchanged (find_modes), and its info has no mixing entry; tests/test_modes_mixing.py guards it.

  • The test chain is the chain the modes are then sampled with: same sampler, walkers and steps. The question is whether that chain mixes; do not shorten it to save time.

  • A walker’s basin is the one it climbs to (assign_basins), not the nearest fitted Gaussian. A Gaussian fitted to the few walkers of a small basin in 58 dimensions gave the basin away (memory 0.00 on basins that exchanged almost nothing, measured).

  • Memory is P(A later | A before) - P(A later | B before) among walkers in A or B later, half a chain apart, squared. Counting crossings or the share of walkers that moved depends on the basins’ weights; the memory does not (2% to 50% tested).

  • find_basins drops nothing. A group under min_share then goes with the large basin it has the smallest dip to when that dip is under barrier, and is otherwise left out and counted; it is never tested as a basin of its own (too few walkers for a memory).

  • Crossings where the straight path dips 10 to 20 log-units are real with the stretch move at moderate dimension (it throws walkers from the inner tail). Do not treat them as a fault of the labelling.

  • client.py modes evaluates the network only and writes only RUN/modes/.

Before changing the boost’s background or region#

acquisition.boost.background and .region (model/network.py, sampling/region.py); read docs/boost_background_region.md. Keep these:

  • Both opt-in. Without them nothing changes, under MPI too: everything they add runs on the master inside steps that exist already.

  • The quadratic is fitted once, at the first training that holds boost points (the first after the boosts’ start; also when that first boost fell back to an ordinary batch), and that training starts from random weights. Afterwards it is read from the previous model (model_background) and never refitted: a network cannot absorb a change of it, so a refit is a cold start.

  • background_fit: least_squares (opt-in) fits the quadratic with the injection’s _fit_quadratic to the rows within background_window · T · ndim / 2 of the best. Keep the negative curvatures as fitted: the injection’s floor at 1e-3 of the largest raises true curvatures of a correlated likelihood in standardised coordinates (529 rms at the posterior of the 58-parameter Gaussian; tests/test_boost_background_region.py catches it). Without the key the crude fit is as it was, and the training domain is built from the rows within T · ndim of the best whichever fit is used.

  • The background alone is not safe on a likelihood that is not Gaussian: where the truth is flatter than the quadratic the network must add a rising correction, and it carries it into the empty corners (the twin: maxima 2000 above the best). Do not recommend it without the region.

  • The region is built from boost rows only, and only from those whose true value confirms them. No network enters it. Outside it the surrogate does not evaluate the network.

  • The audit’s searches are projected onto the region (Region.project_unit), not walled: a wall throws a 58-parameter search about (14 792 end points for 14 976 starts, against one).

  • Current boost groups never share historical rows. Match clouds by verified-row overlap, not the mode number (its weight rank); multiple older clouds can join one current group. An overlap is not enough to join two sets: the mean of the one with fewer rows must lie inside the reach of the other (_one_mode). On any overlap, one stray row merged two modes and tripled a cloud’s reach.

  • A sparse cloud (at most SPARSE x ndim distinct rows) takes the shape of the well-sampled cloud with the most rows (_donor), its own centre, and its own reach capped at the donor’s. Never give it a covariance shrunk towards a sphere in box units, or any shape estimated from its own handful of rows: a real cloud is hundreds of times narrower in some directions, and the shrunk ellipsoid admitted the network at false maxima of the twin (docs/boost_background_region.md has the table). A cloud without a shape (one distinct row; sparse while nothing is well sampled) is left out by boost_region, counted (left_out) and logged. It must not raise in the loop: the error would come from the saved boost files, at every restart. Well-sampled clouds stay bit-identical to the saved regions of earlier runs: after a change, build the regions of a long run’s boost files with the old and the new code and compare them.

  • Region.start(component=k) must find points in any cell, also one that is a small part of its ellipsoid: it closes in on the ellipsoid’s centre. Without a component the draw is the one it always was, number for number, whenever it finds its points in 200 draws (every saved region checked, 224); after that it closes in on the centres too. Until then it raised, and a region reaching far beyond the box (the axion run’s network 18, 0.46% of the draws inside) stopped the loop’s last chain for good.

  • region_quantile and train_domain (17 October 2026; “The training domain and the quantile reach” in the doc) are opt-in and need region. Without them every region is bit-identical to the saved ones (218 regions of 41 runs rebuilt with the code before and after) and the loop trains on every point; keep both true. The quantile changes the region’s reach only, never which rows are one mode (_matching, _one_mode, shaped keep the farthest).

  • The training domain is built once, at the first training after the boosts’ start, from the rows fit_background uses (within T * ndim of the best), and is never rebuilt: a domain that grew with the boosts would bring back the far points and the jumps of the farthest-point reach. Its file is named after the network that built it, so that a branch at the start and a rewind before it leave it behind, and it is a training phase’s output (run/storage.py phase_outputs, the loop’s artifact list). The cut is applied to the training arrays only: the dataset, its files, the acquisition’s density and the derived emulator keep every point. Keep it opt-in; see the measurements and limitations in the doc. Its coverage diagnostic estimates box-clipped union volume with volume/overlap weights and reports effective sample counts. Never use Region.start as uniform volume draws: its equal-cell selection and centre-shrinking fallback serve initialization instead.

  • The region keeps the chains to the known modes. It is for the boost phase and for consumers of a finished network (the evidence and profiles, both by default), never for the tempered acquisition of the ordinary loop.

Before changing the region in the profile code#

The profile setting region (profiles/region.py, model/network.py::restrict_model and RegionWall, profiles/workflow.py); read “Networks trained on boosts” in docs/create_profiles.md. Keep these:

  • The default is run. A network trained on boosts searched over the whole box gave false maxima 40 posterior sigma from the data on a real 49-parameter run and no node converged; do not make the bare network the default again. A network without a region file is searched over the whole box whatever the setting, and that path is bit for bit the one before the setting.

  • What the searches get is a real Keras model at double precision, so every optimiser takes it (the node optimiser copies it with copy_model_dtype). Profile training gets the network itself, never the confined one.

  • The manifest holds the resolved region (file, hash, edge; None for the bare network) and a resume compares that, not configuration.region: a study made before the setting resumes with the default exactly when its network has no region.

  • A derived-coordinate view (derived/coordinates.py) copies the region of its network into VIEW/region/ and records it in view.json (region); never into VIEW/boost/, where the run’s chains, evidence and modes would read it in the view’s coordinates. The region stays in the run’s coordinates: ViewRegion measures a point z of the view at x(z), the pivot solve’s output, and restrict_view puts the wall between the network and the feasibility penalty. The manifest’s entry carries the pivot map (coordinates, file and hash). A view made before 14 October 2026 from a network with a region holds the bare network: the default refuses it (remake the view), region: null uses it as before.

  • A node whose optimum is not inside the region is cut from the end of its range and the scan repeated, before any true call of a node; the cut is recorded in the scan’s file (region_cuts) and in ranges.json. Do not let such a node through to the true stage: its start is on the wall or pressed against it, and the residual Newton stage reads the surrogate’s curvature there.

  • INSIDE (0.98 of the edge) is where a search pressed against the edge ends (rho = 1.0000 in the tests). It is not tuned on anything else.

  • 2D surfaces are searched inside the region, and nodes outside are not cut there: the study stops after the scan with the list (profiles/surface_workflow.py). Do not let them through either; the scan certifies a point of the wall.

  • Measured: one real 49-parameter study with the edge at 1 (735 nodes, 677 converged), and the two-mode twin with the edge at 2 and at 1. The cut has only been exercised on analytic networks. tests/test_profile_region.py covers the wall, the setting, the cut, resumes and both dimensions.

Before changing run/storage.py or run/identity.py#

The run-state layer: phase commits, the run lock, the objective identity; read docs/run_recovery.md. Keep these:

  • A phase without a commit is never taken as done, and a continued run can always get past it: PhaseStore.recover moves the phase’s files to RUN/interrupted/ and the phase is done again. Do not turn that into a refusal: a job at its time limit lands inside a training or an acquisition nearly every time, and a refusal there ends the run. Do not replay journal values into the training set either.

  • phase_outputs must list every file a phase writes before its commit. A file missing from it survives an interruption and is read by the redone phase or by the start-iteration detection (run/run.py), which takes the latest network and training set on disk.

  • Nothing in a run directory is deleted by this layer.

  • A committed file is never rewritten outside the phase that commits it. The region of a loaded network is read from its file for that reason (client.py, “Region of the boosts”); a rebuilt one changed the file’s hash and the next start was refused. The derived emulator’s files are phase outputs that are not committed for the same reason: client.py derived --retrain replaces them on a finished network, as documented, and a hash would refuse the next start.

  • The identity records what decides the value at a point, not the text of CLiENT’s wrapper files or unrelated launcher helpers. Dependencies of the objective must be recorded even beside the launcher; schema migration must not silently trust added or renamed entries. A wrapper change that alters returned values is declared by raising BaseLikelihood.objective_convention in that wrapper. Do not go back to hashing wrapper sources: a comment edit then refuses every run.

  • --accept-identity-change keeps the earlier record and never waives a domain change against metadata.json.

  • hpc/analysis/branch_run.py copies phase records only when every file they commit is copied, and rejects incomplete/damaged retained checkpoints before creating a branch; tests/test_hpc_branch_run.py checks a branch with the real PhaseStore. It reads a file’s iteration as _it_N not followed by a digit (with a dot required, the evidences and the phase history had none and went into every branch), leaves evidence/, modes/, analysis/, interrupted/, phases/history/ and boost/start.json behind, and takes the source’s boost start as the earlier of its settings and boost/start.json. --train-from N is the other kind of branch, inside the boosts: it keeps the source’s start and boost/start.json, takes networks 0 to N - 1 with their committed training and acquisition and the training set of N, and not N’s audit (imports.json says trained_again_from); network N is trained again by the branch.

  • Branches keep complete parent call journals under branch_provenance/source/, outside active objective_calls/rank*.jsonl. imports.json counts inherited finite native coordinate/value pairs from the checkpoint, audit and optional augmentation separately from new objective calls. Never count earlier cumulative training sets again or describe these imports as cache hits. run.objective_calls.summarize runs after every acquisition: a record it cannot read, or a branch from before 293887a (no branch_provenance/), is named in branch_note, never raised.

  • The branch is filled under <name>.partial and renamed last, so a directory that reads as a run is a whole branch (the threshold suite takes the one directory it finds). Before changing profiles/residual_newton.py#

Read the “residual Newton” section and the version history in docs/hybrid_optimisers.md. The method was developed against the real SINU TT likelihood (CLASS++), not only the analytic 31D target, because the two behave differently: the real residual carries ~1e-3 - 2.5e-3 of value noise at probe scale, and the network’s flattest direction (the tau_reio - A_s degeneracy) runs into the tau prior edge within about one network sigma, where the truth drops by tens of log L. Hence the small initial trust radius, the long central-difference baseline along the flattest directions and a tolerance (1e-2) a few times the gradient-noise floor. Judge a change on a network standing in for the truth with added noise of 0.0025 (calls and the worst local gap) and on real CLASS++ nodes, never on the 31D target alone. Results must not depend on workers (tests/test_residual_newton.py checks serial = parallel and resume).

A node whose linear algebra fails (LinAlgError: numpy’s solve raises for a singular system and for one with a NaN in it) ends as singular with its best true point, and passes the state it was given on along its chain; a transfer step that fails is skipped (the axion run’s two A_osc studies each lost their last node, and with it the whole study, in ascend of the corrected model at large amplitude). A node that does not fail is unchanged (tests/test_newton_singular.py). Where the NaN came from was not established.

A node whose start the likelihood refuses (a prior edge, a Boltzmann failure, which the cluster backend turns into -inf) has no value (refused_start: logl and x None, reason “refused start”, flag 1) and passes on the state it was given in the same way, also in 2D; the study goes on, and it stops only if every node is refused. The reports read over the nodes that have a value (profiles/metric.py refuses a NaN; reporting.crossings_with_values) and warn. A checkpoint keeps this on a resume, since the objective is deterministic. Within a study a refused point is also not called again (ConcurrentLedger.refused); a resume asks once more, as it imports finite values only.

A gradient round that ends exactly at the node’s call budget still gets its verdict (the test costs no call; the node was reported out of budget). Both proxy testbeds are unchanged by it, node by node.

Two rules, each with a test there: the curvature bound certifies convergence only after a step inside the trust radius whose point is the incumbent (after a step at the radius, or when a probe is better, a new gradient round follows); and a step without a finite true value is a failed step that does not enter the curvature model. As an excess of -inf it was written in as the stiffest curvature allowed, and the node and those started from its state ran out of budget.

Before changing evidence/#

python client.py evidence (BEST nested sampling of a network); read docs/evidence.md, “Single precision, and when BEST stops”. Keep these:

  • BEST works in single precision and adds ln Z up in it. It is given the network minus a reference near the peak (the best training value, itself a single-precision number, so the difference is exact), and the reference is added back in double precision to ln Z, ln Z_box and the chain’s column. Do not hand BEST raw values of a real likelihood again: at -15656, taken to the end, the sum was 0.46 low with a quoted error of 0.03 (measured; tests/test_evidence_precision.py).

  • --tolerance is the fraction of the evidence left in the live points, and BEST gets its logarithm. BEST’s own tolerance is compared with ln(Z_live / Z_dead): a positive value stops every run at one half.

  • The default is 2048 live points, a power of two: BEST adds the prior volume up in single precision, n_live_updates / n_live at every iteration, and that sum is exact only then (the command says so for another number). Do not go back to a round decimal default.

  • --n-live-updates is a sixteenth of the live points by default (128 of 2048; 10 until 14 October 2026). BEST’s time is its network calls in sequence, and a call of 128 points costs about twice one of 10: 2.5 to 4.3 times faster, the same ln Z on Gaussians with exact evidences, SINU and a 49-parameter network, and the same (BEST’s own, about −0.6) bias on the exact 31-parameter ridge (docs/evidence.md, “Live points replaced per iteration”). A single-thread benchmark of a small network says otherwise; do not judge it on one.

  • The region of the boosts is the default, by the shared resolver (sampling/targets.py); --region none is the bare network. A bare --region must keep meaning “the region, or stop”: the cluster’s job script passes it. --region E is the same region with its edge at E reaches (another target: its output gets _edgeE and stands beside the default one, and the job script leaves its own --region out when the arguments carry one).

  • The summary in hpc/analysis/evidence_summary.py withholds the correction of an evidence whose own posterior (its chain beside the JSON) lies where its true check has no sample: above UNSAMPLED (5%) of it at network values above the highest sample, or farther from the samples’ mean than the farthest, where about 1 / (n + 1) is expected. Without the chain (or the check’s x and names) the correction is made as before. The samples’ own distances are leave-one-out: measured from a mean and covariance they entered, the farthest of 500 in 49 parameters left 2 to 5% of sound networks outside and withheld a correction . Measured: at most 0.5% (higher) and 0.75% (farther) on twelve real networks of 16 to 49 parameters, 99.9% on the SINU branch’s network 21, whose evidence lived on a false corner maximum.

  • The output’s region is the resolved entry or None. Analysis caches record full target provenance (sampling/provenance.py). In that summary an evidence and a true check that both record a target must record the same one, or there is no correction. A result from before targets were recorded is joined by the network’s number and the summary names it: withholding those corrections emptied the table of existing runs, whose evidences take hours each.

  • The summary compares the networks with each other (the difference to the last one, the spread of the corrected evidences) whatever each was integrated over, with a note when that differs: every boost network has its own region, so a rule that compares only equal targets compares nothing, and the comparison is what the table is for. Different prior conventions are not compared. The convention is convention_of: the convention’s name, each prior as defined, the constant that follows from them. Never the whole priors table of an output: its rows also hold the box and the mass sampled at its faces, which differs in any two runs (compared, it blanked every comparison of real files while every test, whose fixtures had no such table, passed). Test the summary on files shaped as client.py evidence writes them (Summary.realistic in tests/test_hpc_evidence.py).

  • Seed-scatter notes need the same target, convention, stopping rule and tolerance, and no capped run. A recorded target that cannot be used is not a legacy result: no correction.

  • Analysis identities belong to verified private snapshots (sampling.targets.load_snapshot), not whatever model is on disk when sampling ends. Keep the loaded network, metadata, resolved region and hash together; a replacement during sampling must never relabel results. Tests exercise real evidence/derived-chain producers and the evidence summary. client.py modes (target in its JSON, target_json in its samples) and the derived view (source_target and source_files in view.json) follow the same rule; a new consumer of a saved network takes it from load_snapshot too.

  • A chain replaced by one of the same target is not archived (the report samples every network again each run); corner.py and make_config.py read posterior_it_N.npz only, never an archived posterior_it_N.previous-<id>.npz.

  • BEST’s sum of ln X is in single precision too, exact only for a power of two of live points. That is BEST’s; it is documented, not corrected.

hpc/analysis/true_check.py cannot run in the unit suite (MPI and a Cobaya likelihood), and a line of it that no test ran once called a string: the job would have stopped after its true evaluations. Keep these:

  • Anything such a program prints or computes from its results is a function that a test calls (report_line, chain_line, network_entry, narrower_than_the_run), not text inside main.

  • After changing it, run it: mpirun -n 2 python hpc/analysis/true_check.py RUN --cobaya input/cobaya/banana_lcdm16_wide.yaml --networks N --points 200 --steps 2000 --discard-chains on a scratch copy of a run of the analytic 16-parameter target (a small run) takes seconds.

  • tests/test_source_compiles.py compiles every file with warnings as errors. A compiler warning is a defect waiting for its line to run; do not silence it.

  • A consumer is checked on files its real producer wrote (WhatTheCommandsWrite in tests/test_hpc_evidence.py), not on files rebuilt for the test.

Environment and tests#

From the repository root in the environment declared by environment.yaml:

PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 \
  python -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_*.py'

The suite includes serial/parallel equivalence, interrupted recovery, boundary-release and failed-probe checks. It uses analytic targets and does not need CLASS++ or Planck likelihood data.

python tests/release_smoke.py runs the whole workflow through the command line, in about three minutes, and python tests/run_mpi_smokes.py the MPI paths. After changing a thing that compares evaluated coordinates with chosen ones, or the launcher, run tests/mpi_train_domain_smoke.py as well, which goes through hpc/launch.py.

Known limits#

  • Networks with several modes. A network whose region has several ellipsoids is sampled cell by cell and weighted by its own evidence in each cell (sampling/mixture.py): by the derived chain, and by the report and true check in hpc/. Do not substitute starting proportions or an older boost’s corrected weights; keep the per-cell diagnostics, and do not use weights the sampler marks as not reliable. Chain caches carry the sampler method and version separately from target_json. Unreliable weights withhold moment, ESS and evidence corrections; retain the true evaluations and pointwise errors. Every reader of a chain asks how it was sampled (hpc/analysis/chain_sampling.py, derived.chain.weights_problem for derived --exact, and the record benchmarking/benchmark.py keeps in its cached chain). The mark is the efficiency of the bridge’s Gaussian mixture as a proposal for the cell, not the chain’s length: do not write “sample longer” as the remedy. Before a real run with several modes, check the cell weights against the boost’s own (boost_it_N.json) on its first networks.

  • The objective identity keeps its scope (run/identity.py: user-site packages and the likelihood’s component files are recorded). A package upgrade therefore stops each continued run once, and --accept-identity-change after a look at the listed differences is the answer; do not narrow the record to avoid that.

  • Profiles. Nodes whose optimum lies at rho 0.9 to 0.98 of the region stall, since their probes cross the wall (profiles/region.py INSIDE); second-order certification is not required for converged in the hybrid scan; the retraining path (retrain: true) raises on designs outside the region.

  • An uncleared audit does not stop the loop. With clear: false the native loop goes on; see docs/posterior_density_boost.md.

  • The validation set under a warm start is drawn afresh at every training. Measured on an analytic target at four seeds, the printed validation loss is optimistic (12% above the training loss where a held-out set is 42% above), and the networks are no worse for it; a persistent set was, if anything, slightly worse. Do not read val_loss as a network’s error. A training of the boost phase from random weights keeps its best epoch unless training.random_start_weights: last (opt-in, 19 November 2026; docs/boost.md). A warm-started training of the boost phase keeps its last weights: with the best ones, most boost-phase trainings kept their first of about 400 epochs. Branches of three runs with last were 0.48 to 0.86 of the originals’ error at true values where those had kept an early epoch, and the same where they had not (client.py, restore_best). Not measured in the ordinary loop, whose networks decide the hand-over (the 1.2 T threshold was measured with best): do not extend it there without that measurement.
