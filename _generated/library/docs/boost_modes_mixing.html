Boost: deciding the modes by measured mixing#

A boost divides the posterior into modes “that a T = 1 chain cannot travel between” and gives each its own chain, its own evidence and the same number of points (boost.md). By default two maxima are one mode when the straight path between them dips less than barrier (4 log-units). Two things are wrong with a fixed number:

  • how high a barrier a chain crosses depends on the number of parameters, and

  • a straight path between two maxima of a curved mode crosses ground the chain never visits.

mixing replaces the number by the thing it stands for: a chain is run and asked whether it travels. It is opt-in; without the key the modes are found by the fixed barrier.

acquisition:
  boost:
    n: 2000
    start_iteration: 15
    mixing: 0.1            # two basins are one mode when a T = 1 chain keeps at most this
                           # much memory of which of them a walker started in
    # candidate_barrier: 1.0   # log-units; maxima closer than this are one basin without a test

The same analysis of any saved network, outside the loop and without the likelihood:

python client.py modes RUN_DIR [-i N] [--mixing M] [--barrier-only] [--tempered-steps S] [--seed S] [--tag T]

What is done#

The first step of a boost is the same: an ensemble started on the tempered chain is cooled to T = 1 and every walker climbs to its local maximum. Then, in place of the grouping by barrier:

  1. Candidate basins. The maxima are grouped as by the fixed rule, but with the low candidate_barrier (1 log-unit: dips under it are the network’s own wiggles, and no chain is needed to know that they are crossed). Nothing is dropped at this stage. A climb that stalled on a slope goes to the basin of the highest maximum it is joined to. A basin with less than min_share of the walkers is too small to stand for itself: it joins the large basin with the smallest dip to it when that dip is under barrier, and is otherwise left out and counted.

  2. One basin: one mode. No chain is run, and the test costs nothing.

  3. A free chain. With several basins, one T = 1 chain of the boost’s own walkers and steps is started on the cooled walkers and left free in the whole box.

  4. Where the walkers are. At eight times in the first half of the chain, and half a chain later, every walker’s position is given a basin by letting it climb: it is in the basin of the maximum it reaches (the basin to one of whose maxima the straight path from there dips least; up to 48 maxima stand for a basin).

  5. Memory. For two basins A and B, among the walkers found in A or B at the later time,

    memory = P(in A later | in A before) - P(in A later | in B before).

    It is 1 when no walker changes basin and 0 when where a walker is no longer depends on where it was. It is measured half a chain apart and squared, which is what is left after the whole chain if the memory decays exponentially.

  6. Decision. Basins whose memory after the chain is at most mixing are one mode; this is applied to every pair, so basins that mix through a third are one mode too. The modes are numbered by size.

Then the boost goes on as with the fixed barrier: one confined chain and one bridge-sampled evidence per mode, the same number of points from each. A mode made of several basins has as its region the union of theirs. The Gaussians that mark the regions are fitted to the cooled walkers and to the labelled positions of step 4 together.

The threshold has a direct meaning. For two basins the memory is also the factor by which an error in the starting split between them has shrunk by the end of the chain. With mixing: 0.1 two basins share a chain when that chain forgets at least nine tenths of its starting split; otherwise their weights are taken from the two evidences, which do not depend on the split at all.

The test is about the chain the boost samples with: the same sampler, number of walkers and number of steps. A longer chain mixes across more, and the answer changes with it. That is intended.

The twin’s exact weights inside its box are 80.9% and 19.1%. Its two modes are joined by a curved valley that rises 6.5 log-units; the chain does not get through it, although a straight barrier of 6 with 16 parameters is crossed freely (first table). What a chain crosses depends on the width and the shape of the way as well as on its height, which is the reason for measuring it.

On the real SINU network both rules find the two modes, in both runs.

  • Weights. The strong mode gets 14.7% and 13.7% with mixing, 13.4% and 13.9% with the fixed rule; three earlier evaluations of this network with the fixed rule gave 13.2%, 13.5% and 13.9%. The largest difference, in the first pair, is 0.11 in the log evidence of the weak mode, where the spreads of the two evidences are 0.05 and 0.07. No difference between the rules shows beyond that scatter.

  • Walkers left out. The fixed rule leaves four and six cooled walkers out (climbs that stalled, or groups under min_share). With mixing a stalled climb stays with the hill it is on, and a small group goes with the large basin it is nearest to (six walkers in the second run).

  • The shoulder on the barrier. The strong mode has a shoulder at log10 G_eff between -2.8 and -2.0, presumably where these walkers sit (their positions were not looked at). With mixing it is sampled as part of the strong mode and holds 0.4% and 0.3% of the posterior in the two runs. With the fixed rule it holds under 0.1% and 0.3%: there it depends on where the boundary between the two regions happens to fall. Whether the true likelihood has the shoulder is not known from this (boost.md notes an island at the same place in network 16 that was gone in network 17). An unfiltered boost with mixing would put 2 to 3% of the strong mode’s points on it, which is where true values would settle it.

  • Memory. 0.99 in one run and 0.89 in the other, 9 and 62 positions of 4096 having changed basin. Probably these are walkers on the shoulder, which can go either way (not checked). Both values are far from the threshold.

  • Memory near 0: the chain has forgotten where its walkers started, and the basins are sampled by one chain. Near 1, as here: hardly a walker changed basin. Values between say that walkers do cross but too rarely for the chain’s split to be trusted.

  • Walker positions half a chain apart are the pairs behind the estimate (at most eight times the number of walkers). A basin with few of them has a noisy memory; near the threshold the decision can then go either way. Neither answer is wrong there: a chain that half mixes gives a usable split, and so do two evidences.

  • The straight path between their maxima is printed for comparison with the fixed rule. It can be far above what the chain has to cross (the ring: 23 against 2).

  • A further line gives the time the test took.

  • The report (RUN/boost/boost_it_N.json under search.mixing, or RUN/modes/modes_it_N.json) has the candidate basins, every pair’s memory at half the chain and after it, the number of exchanges, the walkers of small groups that were joined to a basin and those left out, the seconds, and on_no_known_hill: positions of the free chain that climbed to a hill none of the known maxima is on (they are given the nearest basin).

The modes command#

python client.py modes RUN_DIR runs the tempered chain of the run’s sampling block on a saved network (the latest, or -i N), then exactly what a boost does first, and stops before any true evaluation. It reads the run’s acquisition.boost settings when there are any, and uses the region that belongs to that network (the latest saved at or before its iteration) when the run has one. It decides by mixing (--mixing; by default the run’s own mixing, or 0.1 when the run has none) unless --barrier-only is given. It writes

  • RUN_DIR/modes/modes_it_N[_TAG].json: for every mode the weight, the evidence and its spread, the peak, the mean and standard deviation of every parameter; the mixing test pair by pair; and target, the identity of what was searched (the hashes of the network and the metadata, the domain, the region with its hash and edge), as the chains and evidences record it;

  • RUN_DIR/modes/modes_it_N[_TAG].npz: the T = 1 samples of every mode (x0, f0, x1, …), weights, names and the same identity (target_json). Together they are the network’s posterior: each mode’s samples carry its weight.

The network, the metadata and the region are loaded from one verified private copy (sampling.targets.load_snapshot): a network written again while the search runs, by a training that ends, is not the one the result is recorded for.

This is also the way to get the weights of a finished run’s modes, which the loop itself does not report after its last iteration. It needs no MPI and changes no file of the run outside RUN_DIR/modes/.

On a trained network most of it is probably the climbs (each of the 8192 positions climbs for 600 steps and is then compared with up to 48 maxima of every basin), judging from what the other chains of that length cost; that part has not been tuned. The time is printed in the log and stored in the report (seconds). It is spent on the master while the MPI workers sleep, as the rest of the mode search.

What is not established#

  • The candidate basins still come from straight paths between maxima, with a low threshold. A basin none of the cooled walkers climbed to is not a candidate; positions of the free chain found on such a hill are counted (on_no_known_hill) and not acted on.

  • The memory is squared from half a chain to the whole on the assumption that it decays exponentially. For two basins with one crossing rate it does; for a chain that first crosses quickly from the barrier’s edge and then slowly from the cores it underestimates the memory that is left.

  • One threshold, 0.1, was used throughout. The decisions above change between memories of 0.03 and 0.15, so anything from about 0.05 to 0.15 gives the same table.

  • The sampler of the chain is the loop’s ensemble sampler with the stretch move. A network sampled afterwards with another sampler mixes differently.

  • More modes at more parameters means fewer points for each from a boost of n. With the three-maximum ring at 58 parameters each of three modes gets a third.

  • On its own in the loop it has been run for two boosts only, on the analytic twin with short settings (the boost, its report, the region built from the modes, the next training and audit): a check that the pieces fit, not a study. It has not been studied on a real likelihood: the real branches of boost.md that use it changed it together with other settings.

  • With region the surrogate is a wall outside the region. When the straight path between two maxima leaves the region, the dip printed is the wall’s and says nothing about the chain: in the second boost of that short run two basins with 4161 log-units between them by the straight path had a memory of 0.03 and were merged.

  • A boost with mixing can change the number of modes from one boost to the next (basins that begin to mix are merged). The region of acquisition.boost.region copes with that by construction (an earlier cloud joins the later boost’s mode that it overlaps and whose reach its mean lies in, several clouds into one where a later boost finds one mode; see boost_background_region.md), but on its own that combination has only been exercised in the short run above.
