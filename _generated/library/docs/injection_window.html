Injection: fitting the quadratic to the points near the best#

prior.n_inject and acquisition.n_inject add points drawn from the Gaussian that a quadratic fit of the training set implies, at the acquisition temperature T. They are meant to land about T n / 2 log-units below the peak (n parameters), where the tempered chain is.

On real CMB likelihoods in a wide training box they do not when the quadratic is fitted to every training point: it puts them thousands of log-units below the peak. Both injections fit only the points near the best, within 16 x T n / 2 log-units (INJECT_WINDOW in dataset/peak_samples.py):

prior:
  n_inject: 100
acquisition:
  n_inject: 100

The window does nothing while both n_inject are 0 (their default).

What goes wrong#

Without the window the quadratic is fitted by least squares to every training point. A log-likelihood is quadratic near its peak, not over a box a hundred posterior widths across, where the training values span tens of thousands of log-units. On two laptop runs (Planck + DESI, ΛCDM with 15 parameters and self-interacting neutrinos with 16) the fit to every point had:

  • a residual of 12 000 to 43 000 log-units;

  • its own peak value 7 000 to 48 000 above the best true value;

  • 5 to 8 of its 15 or 16 curvatures with the wrong sign;

  • its vertex 2 to 15 posterior widths (rms over the parameters) from the posterior mean.

The loop prints this in every such run, for instance

Injecting 100 points from the quadfit Gaussian at T=7: fitted on 1996 points, vertex value 64583.1, ... fit rms 43214.3
   injected log-likelihood range [-15354.3, -14086.1]

where the best value of that run is about -5300. The same is seen on a cluster run (ΛCDM, 7 October: the injected points of iterations 0 to 2 lie 8812, 4937 and 8267 below the best). On the analytic banana the fit to every point works, in a box of 10 and of 30 widths.

What the window does#

  1. Which points. Only those within 16 × T n / 2 log-units of the best training value are fitted. If that leaves fewer than three points per coefficient of the quadratic (1 + n + n (n + 1) / 2 coefficients), the best three per coefficient are used.

  2. How wide. The Gaussian is drawn with variance T / curvature along every eigenvector of the fitted Hessian, but never wider along an eigenvector than the fitted points themselves are spread along it. Where a curvature is near zero or has the wrong sign, T / |curvature| is a width the points say nothing about.

The fit routine, the stratified draw and the rejection at the box are the same with and without the window. The log line names the points fitted, the wrong-sign curvatures and the directions along which the width was capped.

Both final networks are 0.06 log-units rms from the truth at 500 samples of their own posterior, and the two posteriors agree to 0.013 σ in every mean and 0.8% in every width. Before the boosts the run with the window was the better one: its network 10 was 0.69 from the truth at posterior samples against 1.03 (220 effective points of 500 against 79), its network 9 had 2.9 against 3.4 in the band 15 to 52 below the best, and it stayed about one boost ahead (0.14 against 0.25 after one boost, 0.06 against 0.10 after three). One seed each: that lead cannot be told from the scatter between two runs. The run without the window spent its 1100 injected evaluations 4 300 to 9 400 below the peak.

Measurements on saved runs#

Depth of the 100 injected points below the best true value of the whole run: median (10%, 90%). A correct Gaussian fit gives T n / 2 ± T √(n / 2).

For the points the runs themselves injected the true values are known. Every other depth is the run’s last network evaluated at the drawn points, standing in for the truth: on its own training points that network is good to 3 to 18 log-units rms within 2000 of the best and useless beyond, so a depth under 2000 is reliable and a larger one only says “far”. The stand-in reproduces the true depths of the runs’ own injections (third against fourth column).

What was tried and not adopted#

  • Other windows (4, 8, 16, 32 times T n / 2; the best 2, 3, 5 points per coefficient). Late in a ΛCDM run they all give the same. Early in a run none is best throughout: 16 is far better than 4 or 8 at ΛCDM iteration 2 (150 against 770) and SInu iteration 1 (580 against 3300), and worse than 4 at SInu iteration 2 (1200 against 280). 32 is worse on ΛCDM (iteration 2: 1750). Small fixed counts are erratic on SInu.

  • Choosing the window by a hold-out (fit without the best fifth of the points, predict them): it does not pick the window whose points land best.

  • Drawing the points in four rounds with a refit between them. On ΛCDM it reaches the peak faster in the first iterations (best point 430 below the run’s best after iteration 0, against 934 in one round). On SInu it is erratic: some rounds land 20 000 below (emulated with the stand-in).

What is not established#

  • Whether the injected points help at all on a real likelihood. In the pair of runs above the final networks do not differ, and the lead of the run with the window before the boosts is within what two runs of one configuration might differ by; that scatter was not measured. No run without any injection was made on that likelihood. The evidence that injection speeds convergence is from analytic targets, where the fit to every point works.

  • One true-likelihood test, on ΛCDM, one seed. On the non-Gaussian likelihood the restricted fit has only been judged with the stand-in network.

  • One window (16) on two real likelihoods and one box. A narrower training box makes the fit to every point less wrong and the window less needed.

  • The curved-degeneracy caveat of UPGRADING.md (a quadratic puts its vertex off a parabolic ridge) is about the shape near the peak and is not changed by the window.

Files#

  • dataset/peak_samples.py: near_best, gaussian_peak_samples(..., window=). _fit_quadratic is shared with the profile code: the window selects the points before the fit and caps the widths after it.

  • dataset/peak_samples.py: INJECT_WINDOW (16). client.py: draw_peak_samples passes it on and prints what was fitted.

  • tests/test_injection_window.py: the selection, the default left alone, a target with a Gaussian peak and one-sided walls where the fit to every point fails as on the real runs, the width cap, the configuration.
