Several trainings per network (training.members)#

training.members: K trains every network of the boost phase K times on the same training set with the same settings and keeps one. Rank 0 trains it as before (warm-started from the previous network when training.warm_start is on, the default). K - 1 members are trained from random weights, each with its own seed, on ranks that would otherwise wait for the next evaluation: during a training only rank 0 works, and on a cluster the other ranks’ cores are idle for the whole of it. Members left over when the run has fewer ranks than members are trained on rank 0 after its own network. The members from random weights keep the epoch of their lowest validation loss, or their last weights with training.random_start_weights: last (boost.md).

It is a feature of the boosts. The members act from the first training on boost points (the network after the boosts’ start, acquisition.boost.start_iteration or the hand-over of tempered_batch_scatter) on; every network of the tempered phase, the hand-over’s included, is trained once, as without the key, and a run without acquisition.boost that sets it is refused. The log says so at the start (training.members: K trainings of every network trained on boost points).

training:
  members: 4              # 1 (the default): one training per network, as before
  selection_share: 0.1    # of the points acquired since the previous training, held out to choose
  member_margin: 0.2      # the warm network is kept unless another is better by more than this
  member_threads: 8       # TensorFlow threads of a member (default TF_NUM_INTRAOP_THREADS, else 4)

How one is chosen#

  • The selection set. A share of the points acquired since the previous training (its boost or batch and its injection; the previous network’s audit points are excluded, since the warm start has seen them) is held out from every member’s training this time. Every member, the warm one included, is judged on points it has not seen; a warm network’s own validation loss is partly in-sample. The points are trained on from the next network on. The draw has its own generator, so the run’s other draws are unchanged.

  • The choice. The selection loss is the training loss (msre) on the selection set. The warm network is kept unless a member from random weights has a selection loss lower by more than member_margin; without a warm network (warm start off, or the first training after a background fit) the lowest is kept. A member that fails is never kept and is named in the log.

  • What is kept. The chosen network becomes trained_models/model_it_N.keras and its history training_history/history_it_N.csv; the audit, the region, the boost and every later use see only it, and the next warm start starts from it. Every member is saved as trained_models/members/model_it_N_memberK.keras with training_history/history_it_N_memberK.csv (member 0 is rank 0’s), and trained_models/members/members_it_N.json records the seeds, the starts, the epochs, the losses and which was kept and why. These are files of the training phase (run.storage.phase_outputs). The run’s log has one line per training (Members (...)).

The choice by true evaluations (training.member_pilot)#

training:
  members: 12
  member_pilot: 100         # points of each member's own posterior, evaluated with the true likelihood
  member_pilot_steps: 20000 # of its chain (T = 1, inside the network's region)
  member_pilot_walkers: 256 # at least 2 ndim (raised to that when lower)
  member_pilot_depth: null  # default: the msre scale 0.5 chi^2(sigma_level, ndim) below the best
  member_pilot_score: corrected # the rms without each member's constant offset; rms: with it (before 19 November 2026)

With member_pilot: n > 0 the members are chosen after the region is built, by true evaluations, and nothing is held out:

  • The points. Every member samples its own posterior at T = 1 inside the region of rank 0’s network (acquisition.boost.region; without a region, from the best distinct training points), each on the rank that trained it, and n of its points are drawn. The pool (n times the members that could be sampled) is evaluated once with the true likelihood on all ranks; the calls are journalled under the stage members/itN.

  • The score. A member’s pilot error is the rms of its prediction minus the truth over the pool’s points that the truth or that member puts within member_pilot_depth of the best value known (the training set and the pool), after its constant offset has been removed (member_pilot_score: corrected, the default). The offset is the median of the member’s error over the pool’s points that the truth puts within the depth, the same points for all members (0 when there are none). Points whose true value is not finite, or is the fixed value a launcher gives a point the likelihood cannot evaluate (refused_value), count for none. A member that puts a false plateau above the truth is judged where it puts its posterior; a member that misses a peak is judged where the truth is high. Points far below the best in both count for none. member_pilot_score: rms keeps the offset in the score, as before 19 November 2026.

  • The choice. As by the selection loss: the warm network is kept unless another member’s pilot error is lower by more than member_margin; without a warm network the lowest is kept.

  • The points afterwards. Those with a finite true value join the training set before the audit, as the acquisition’s do (they are true evaluations at points where some network puts its posterior). They are saved in trained_models/members/pilot_it_N.csv with the member that drew each, the truth, the derived parameters (derived:NAME) and every member’s prediction; a run continued from the saved network N adds them again. members_it_N.json has choice: pilot, pilot_score, every member’s pilot_error (the score used), pilot_rms, pilot_offset with its standard error and its mean error at its own points (pilot_own_bias), and the kept network’s offset (kept_offset, kept_offset_error). The offset does not change a posterior or a profile; it shifts an evidence of that network by its full value. It is recorded, not applied. hpc/analysis/member_pilot_scores.py RUN scores a run’s pilots again from these files with both scores.

Why: the selection loss judges the members where the previous network put its posterior, and a loss averaged over points does not see where a member will put its own. On network 8 of branches with members: 12 of the two axion monodromy runs below, the member kept by the selection loss was 35.6 (H0WC) and 3.2 (PDP+) too high on average at the points of its own pilot chain (a chain held on a false plateau weighs it more than a boost does: at the two networks’ boost points the means were +1.4 and +0.2); the H0WC one then repeated the setback in its boost (scatter 4.0 at the boost points against 2.1-2.2 for every member from random weights at the same points). The pilot chooses member 5 (error 1.8) and member 7 (1.5, against 4.3 for the kept one); on PDP+ the rank correlation between the selection loss and the pilot error was 0.19.

Cost: n times K true evaluations per network (1200 for n = 100 and K = 12), on all ranks, and one chain per member, run in parallel on the members’ ranks.

Members from random weights had false maxima of their own at networks 12 and 13 (two each, 32-115 too high). At the other runs’ boost points these networks are hardly worse than the rest (at network 10, 1.30-1.60 against 1.22-1.48): the boost points do not see the false maxima, the pilot does. Every choice from 8 to 12 rejected the warm network, and the run avoided the setbacks of the warm-started run (8 and 9) and of the run from random weights (12).

From network 13 on the warm start carried the resolved state on: the warm network was kept at 13 and 15-17, and at 14 member 7 replaced it (pilot error 0.23 against 0.38; 0.21-0.27 against 0.28-0.42 at the boost points of every run). The run had at network 17 the error the run from random weights reached at 18 (0.14); the warm-started one stayed at 1.3.

  • Ranking among sound members. Where no member has a false structure, the pilot ranks the members roughly as the boost points do (rank correlation 0.75-0.83 at H0WC 14, 0.55-0.73 at PDP+ 15). Where one has a false maximum, its pilot points can decide the ranking of the others: at H0WC network 11 three members put the rejected warm member’s points 13-23 too high, inside the depth, which alone gave them pilot errors of 1.8-2.0 (1.08-1.12 without those points, two of them the lowest). The member kept had a scatter of 1.37 at its boost points, against 1.19 for the best of the three. The score prefers a network without a raised region about 20 below the best, which a profile would show, to one slightly better at the posterior’s points. Scoring the others without the points of a member already rejected has not been tried.

  • The margin. On PDP+ the warm network was kept at 10-14 and 16 and replaced at 8 (pilot error 3.55 against 0.90), 9 (0.60 against 0.46) and 15 (0.24 against 0.19). At 9 the warm member was slightly better at the boost points of every run, at 15 the two were equal: for networks this close a margin of 0.2 is inside the pilot’s noise. A margin above 0.39 would have kept the warm network at H0WC 14, which was worse at the boost points of every run. The run ended level with the warm-started one (0.22 at network 15) and below the one from random weights (0.35).

Why several trainings#

A training ends in one of two states: it resolves the fine structure of the likelihood or it stays on a plateau where that structure is smoothed out, and the random start decides which. Retrained on the saved training sets of the H0WC run above, every training sat on the same plateau at epoch 100; on the training set of network 13 one random start in three left it, on that of network 14 two in three, with an error at posterior points none had seen of 0.6-1.0 against 3.9-4.0 for the others. A longer or more patient learning-rate schedule did not help: it memorised the training set. The validation loss separated the two states (2-3e-4 against 1.2-1.3e-3). A warm start keeps the state it inherits, so a network that smoothed the structure passes it on; warm starts are nevertheless better on almost every likelihood tested, which is why rank 0 keeps its warm start and the extra members start from random weights. With a chance p of resolving per start, K starts miss with probability (1 - p)^K: 0.20 for p = 1/3 and K = 4.

When the training set has too few points where the true posterior lies, no start resolves the structure (none of 8 on that run’s network 11); selection cannot help there, and more data can (with 10 000 points from the final posterior added, both starts did).

Cost#

The members train while rank 0 trains its own, so an iteration’s training takes as long as the slowest of them (the schedules end at different epochs). Each member uses member_threads cores and its own memory (the training set, about 10 MB for 100 000 points of 23 parameters, and its network). On the two axion runs above (96 ranks, 12 members at 8 threads) an epoch took as long per training point as in the continuation with one training from random weights (H0WC) or 5% longer (PDP+): the members hardly slowed each other down. The training took 26% (H0WC, networks 9-17) and 35% (PDP+, 9-15) longer than there, and the iteration (training, pilot, audit and a boost of 10 000) 15% and 20% longer. The training sets were 2-8% larger (the pilot’s points), and the slowest member trained on average 14% (H0WC) and 15% (PDP+) more epochs than the median member of the same training, 57% more at H0WC network 12, where a member that resolved the structure trained longest. The pilot’s 1200 true evaluations took 250 s (H0WC) and 100 s (PDP+), against 1950 s and 790 s for a boost of 10 000. The warm-started continuations are no reference for the time: their steps were faster per point than in either of the other two.

This holds while every member has cores of its own: at most 1 + (cores - rank 0’s threads) / member_threads members, 12 on two 48-core nodes at 8 threads and 6 on one. With more, the members share cores, or the ones left over train on rank 0 one after another, and the training takes several times as long. The log then has a warning after the members’ line (members.oversubscription), which takes the run’s cores as its ranks (one core per rank, as a cluster job usually starts them) or the cores of rank 0’s node if those are more. Replayed on the first five choices of the two runs, 6 members (the warm one and every set of 5 of the 11 others) made the same decisions as 12, and the warm network with a false peak was rejected with 2: more members help only where few trainings from random weights resolve the structure. With 6 the slowest member would have trained 10-11% more epochs than the median on average and with 4 8%, against 14-15% with 12: fewer members save little time while each has cores of its own.

With members: 1 nothing is held out, no rank is asked and the run is as before, bit for bit (tests/mpi_members_smoke.py).

On GPUs#

training:
  member_device: auto     # a GPU when the member's process sees one, else the CPU; cpu; gpu (stop without one)

Only rank 0 and the ranks that train members run TensorFlow (utils/devices.py). Each of them takes one GPU of its node, the TensorFlow processes of a node in turn over its GPUs, with memory growth on. The turn is a process’s place among the node’s TensorFlow processes, not among all its ranks: the member ranks are spread at an even stride (8, 16, …, 88 of 96), and by the rank on the node every one of them took GPU 0 of two. Without it, the first TensorFlow process on a GPU reserves nearly all of its memory, and the members started after it there fail. Every other rank hides the GPUs from itself before the likelihood is built (CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES and HIP_VISIBLE_DEVICES empty), so that a likelihood code importing TensorFlow or JAX takes none; the launcher (hpc/submit_client.sh) no longer does it, and a run started with a plain mpirun behaves the same.

training.member_device decides for the members. With auto, the default, a member trains on a GPU when its process sees one and on the CPU otherwise; cpu keeps the members on the CPU; gpu stops a member that sees no GPU (it fails and is never kept). Rank 0 uses a GPU whenever it sees one. On a GPU, member_threads are the CPU threads of what TensorFlow keeps on the CPU, and the oversubscription warning counts only the members left over for rank 0.

The log says where rank 0 trains (TensorFlow on rank 0: GPU 0 (...) or CPU) and, after each training, where every member did (members trained on: 0-11 GPU 0 (...)); members_it_N.json records each member’s device.

tests/gpu_members_smoke.py checks on a GPU node that rank 0 and the members train on the GPUs (both GPUs of a node with two), that none fails, and that member_device: cpu keeps the members on the CPU. benchmarking/gpu_training.py trains one network of a run again on a GPU with TF32 (TensorFlow’s default on Ampere and newer GPUs, for every training there, rank 0’s included), on a GPU in full float32 and on the CPU, from the same random weights, and compares the times and the errors at that network’s boost points.

Measured on Grendel’s NVIDIA L40S nodes (TensorFlow 2.19 with its CUDA wheels):

  • The members on one GPU. Rank 0 and three members trained on the same GPU at once, and none failed; the pilot’s chains ran on it too, and member_device: cpu kept the members on the CPU.

  • A network of the PDP+ axion run (network 10, 61 610 training points, the run’s full schedule, one seed). On the GPU in full float32 the training followed the CPU’s exactly: the same 506 epochs, the same validation loss and the same errors at the 10 000 boost points (scatter 0.372), in 259 s against 1284 s on 8 CPU threads. With TF32 it took 242 s and ended elsewhere, as another seed would (scatter 0.345, inside the 0.29 to 0.59 of that network’s twelve members). TF32 therefore stays as TensorFlow has it.

  • A node with two L40S. With the GPU chosen by the rank on the node, every process took GPU 0 (job 7030123). With the choice by the place among the TensorFlow processes, rank 0 and the three members took GPU 0, 1, 0, 1, the pilot ran on both and no member failed, and member_device: cpu kept the members on the CPU and rank 0 on its GPU (job 7030203).

Open: how many members share one GPU before they slow each other down (rank 0 and three members shared one L40S, on a small network).

Tests#
