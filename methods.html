Scientific methods#

Let \(\ell(x)\) denote the native log-objective actually returned by the backend, and \(s_\theta(x)\) its network approximation inside the saved domain. The objective may include priors. Preserve that convention when training, comparing fits or computing evidence.

Tempered acquisition and the posterior#

A chain sampling the tempered surrogate targets

\[p_T(x) \propto \exp[s_\theta(x)/T].\]

For samples of that distribution, the unnormalised importance weight to its own T=1 surrogate target is

\[w(x) \propto \exp[(1-1/T)s_\theta(x)].\]

If the chain stores \(\log p_T=s_\theta/T\) up to a constant, this is \(\exp[(T-1)\log p_T]\). Subtract the largest log-weight before exponentiation. These weights change the temperature; they do not correct network error.

The Kish effective sample size is

\[N_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{(\sum_i w_i)^2}{\sum_i w_i^2}.\]

It measures weight concentration and is not correlation-adjusted. Inspect autocorrelation and mode exploration separately. The identically named loss-weight ESS (training.msre_ess_floor) is computed over training weights and answers another question.

Profiles#

For coordinates of interest \(q\) and nuisance coordinates \(\eta\),

\[\ell_{\mathrm{prof}}(q)=\max_{\eta:(q,\eta)\in\mathcal D}\ell(q,\eta).\]

The surrogate stage solves the analogous problem for \(s_\theta\). The true stage starts from useful surrogate solutions and constructs a local residual model,

\[m(x)=s_\theta(x)+r_b+g_r^T(x-x_b) +\tfrac12(x-x_b)^TQ_r(x-x_b),\qquad r=\ell-s_\theta.\]

True probes measure the residual; surrogate curvature provides geometry for local steps. Accepted reported values come from the true backend, not from \(m\). Boundary probes, trust regions, continuation and stopping are described in the solver guide.

A plot of \(2[\ell_{\max}-\ell_{\mathrm{prof}}(q)]\) is a native-objective difference. Call it likelihood-only \(\Delta\chi^2\) only if the objective convention justifies that label. A local stop flag does not establish a global maximum or confidence coverage.

Boosts and separated modes#

For mode region \(R_k\), define surrogate and true integrals with the same measure and domain:

\[\widehat Z_k=\int_{R_k}e^{s_\theta(x)}\,dx,\qquad Z_k=\int_{R_k}e^{\ell(x)}\,dx.\]

If \(x\) is sampled from the normalised surrogate restricted to that mode, then

\[Z_k=\widehat Z_k\, \mathbb E_{x\sim\widehat p_k}\!\left[e^{\ell(x)-s_\theta(x)}\right].\]

This motivates true evaluations of posterior draws in each detected mode. The identity does not guarantee an accurate finite-sample estimate: concentrated correction weights or missed regions can defeat it. Density-filtered boost rows are selected points, not those unfiltered posterior draws; the unweighted formula must not be applied to them.

Read boost derivation, mixing-based grouping, and density-filter qualifications.

Evidence and the integration measure#

For a normalised physical prior \(\pi(x)\), Bayesian evidence is \(Z=\int L(x)\pi(x)\,dx\). A uniformly normalised training box, a transformed coordinate prior and a native backend posterior can have different measures. Additive objective constants matter for evidence even when they cancel from a relative profile. The evidence guide specifies the conversion and recorded prior ranges.

Batch error and the handover test#

Let \(\widehat\ell_k\) be the network used to choose an ordinary acquisition batch \(B_k\), and let \(\ell\) be the native backend log objective. On the \(m\) valid pairs, define

\[ e_i=\widehat\ell_k(x_i)-\ell(x_i),\qquad \bar e=\frac1m\sum_i e_i,\qquad s_k=\sqrt{\frac1m\sum_i(e_i-\bar e)^2},\qquad r_k=\sqrt{\frac1m\sum_i e_i^2}. \]

The implementation uses population scatter (denominator \(m\)), and also records \(\max_i|e_i|\). Nonfinite predictions/returns and an explicitly supplied refused-value sentinel are left out. The computation is in float64. These errors are measured before fitting on \(B_k\) and before ordinary injection is appended. Their reuse adds no evaluations, but adaptive batch selection means they are not a representative independent posterior test.

For configured consecutive count \(q\), temperature \(T\) and threshold \(a\), the verdict at network \(N\) is

\[ V_N=\max_{k=N-q,\ldots,N-1}\frac{s_k}{T},\qquad \text{pass only if every measurement exists and }V_N<a. \]

The default for this opt-in metric is \(q=2\), \(a=1.2\). A constant objective offset contributes to RMS but not scatter, motivating scatter for the ordinary-loop decision. That does not make relative offsets between separated modes harmless: they affect mode weights and need mode-specific checks. The reported criterion is a handover heuristic supported by the measured suite, not a bound on posterior or evidence error.

For genuinely unfiltered draws from a normalised surrogate posterior on the same domain and with the same prior convention, a separate identity gives

\[ Z_{\rm true}/Z_{\rm network} =\mathbb E_{p_{\rm network}}\!\left[\exp(\ell-\widehat\ell)\right]. \]

hpc/analysis/evidence_summary.py estimates this from saved posterior true checks. Finite samples, correlation, missed support and low weight ESS limit the estimate. Density-filtered boost rows have a different selection distribution and cannot be inserted into this expectation uncorrected.

Choosing between members#

With training.members, members \(k=0,\dots,K-1\) (\(k=0\) the warm-started network) are trained on the same set. With training.member_pilot each draws \(n\) points of its own T=1 posterior inside the region, and the pool \(P\) of all \(nK\) points is evaluated once with the true objective. Member \(k\)’s pilot error is the rms, without its constant offset \(b_k\), over the points that the truth or that member puts within the depth \(d\) of the best value \(\ell_{\rm best}\) known (training set and pool):

\[ \epsilon_k^2=\frac{1}{|P_k|}\sum_{x\in P_k}\bigl[s_k(x)-\ell(x)-b_k\bigr]^2,\qquad P_k=\{x\in P:\ \ell(x)>\ell_{\rm best}-d\ \ \text{or}\ \ s_k(x)>\ell_{\rm best}-d\}, \]
\[ b_k=\operatorname{median}_{x\in P_\ell}\bigl[s_k(x)-\ell(x)\bigr],\qquad P_\ell=\{x\in P:\ \ell(x)>\ell_{\rm best}-d\}, \]

with \(d\) the msre scale \(\tfrac12\chi^2(\sigma,n_{\rm dim})\) by default. The offset is taken over the same points \(P_\ell\) for every member, and is zero when \(P_\ell\) is empty. A constant changes no posterior or profile, only the evidence, by its full value; member_pilot_score: rms sets \(b_k=0\), the score before 19 November 2026, and the kept network’s \(b_k\) is recorded. A false plateau of member \(k\) is judged where \(k\) puts its posterior, a missed peak where the truth is high. The warm network is kept unless \(\min_{k>0}\epsilon_k<(1-m)\,\epsilon_0\), with margin \(m\) (member_margin); without a warm network the lowest \(\epsilon_k\) is kept. The pool also contains the points of a rejected member, which can decide the ranking of the others: members that agree at the posterior’s points can differ there by a raised region well below the best, which a profile would show. The members from random weights keep the epoch of their lowest validation loss, or with training.random_start_weights: last the weights at the end of their schedule, as the warm network does.
