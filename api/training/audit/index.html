training.audit#

Audit of a trained network’s optima with true evaluations (acquisition.audit).

A network can predict a region far from every training point to be much better than the best true value seen. The acquisition chain starts its walkers uniformly over the box, so it can find such a region; the next batch is then drawn there, and its true values, all poor, are what corrects the network. The audit does the same correction with a few true evaluations instead of a batch:

  1. find_maxima: the network’s highest maxima over the box (the network alone)

  2. flag those predicted more than tau log-units above the best true value

  3. evaluate the true likelihood at the k highest flagged maxima

  4. add them to the training set, fine-tune, and search again

The truth decides: a refuted maximum becomes an ordinary training point where the network was wrong, a confirmed one is a better point than any seen. See docs/audit_optima.md for the measurements behind the defaults and for what the audit cannot do.

training.audit.find_maxima(model, lower, upper, inputs, targets, starts=0, steps=400, learning_rate=0.02, n_top=128, merge=0.05, keep=50, seed=None, counts=None, project=None)#

The network’s highest maxima over the box, by projected gradient ascent.

Parameters:

project – optional TensorFlow function on box-normalised points, applied to the starts and after every step. The ascent then stays on what it projects onto (a Region’s project_unit: the region the boosts’ posterior points reach, sampling/region.py), and the far starts become starts all over its surface.

Starts: starts uniform points (0: default_starts), as many random box vertices, and the n_top best training points. The ascent runs in box-normalised coordinates (Adam, projected onto the box). End points closer than merge box widths (per root dimension) count as one maximum; the keep highest are returned, best first, as dicts with:

x          the point
value      the network's prediction there
excess     value minus the best training value
distance   to the nearest training point, in box widths
at_bound   number of coordinates at a bound
hits       number of starts that ended at this maximum
starts     number of starts in all (hits / starts: the share of the search
           that found it, from which the starts needed to find it follow)

counts, a dict, is filled with the whole search: starts, and for every distinct maximum (not only the keep highest) its excess and hits, highest first.

The vertex starts matter: the most violent false maxima of a long-trained network sit at vertices, and uniform starts alone missed those of two networks out of six. The search is not exhaustive. Four times the starts and twice the steps found one or two further false maxima on two audited networks, with one seed and not with another.

training.audit.summit_maxima(model, summits, lower, upper, inputs, targets, merge=0.05, keep=50, counts=None)#

Points that another search climbed to, as find_maxima returns its maxima: the summits of a tempered chain’s cooled walkers (sampling.modes.cooled_peaks). They are taken where they are. Climbed again by find_maxima, whose ascent takes steps of a fixed size and returns where it ends, a narrow summit was lost: handed a maximum 16 above the best true value, it came back with 9.6.

training.audit.fine_tune(model, inputs, targets, loss, validation, epochs, learning_rate, batch_size)#

Continue training model; the final weights are kept, not the best-validation ones.

Restoring the best validation loss would undo the correction: the validation points never include the audit points, so the loss on them is lowest before the network has learned those.

Compiled without XLA: a fine-tune compiles a new step, on a training set of a new size, and every XLA compilation leaves memory mappings that the process never gives back (about 600 per fine-tune of a 5 x 512 network, measured). With up to eight fine-tunes per iteration that is most of what a run accumulates, and runs of 21 iterations ended at Linux’s limit of 65 530 mappings per process (“LLVM compilation error: Cannot allocate memory”). Without XLA a fine-tune leaves about 20 and takes 10 to 25% longer.

training.audit.audit_optima(model, inputs, targets, validation_split, lower, upper, evaluate, build_loss, config, batch_size, n_append, seed=None, log=print, penalties=(), project=None, peaks=None, cloud=False)#

Run the audit on model, in place. Returns (inputs, targets, derived, report).

Parameters:

project – optional, passed to every search (find_maxima): with a Region’s project_unit the searches stay inside the region of the boosts, so only maxima that a T = 1 consumer could meet are looked for, evaluated and trained on.

peaksoptional (acquisition.audit.chain, in a boost iteration). peaks(model) ->

(summits, walkers): where the walkers of a tempered chain of the model end when cooled and climbed, and the cooled walkers themselves (sampling.modes.cooled_peaks), as the mode search of a boost finds its modes. A round whose own climbs flag nothing looks there last (the “chain” search): a maximum that only the chain reaches was otherwise met by the boost, at a third of its points. A chain per such round.

cloudwith peaks, a flagged maximum of the chain search is evaluated together with

cooled walkers that climbed to it, up to the round’s k points: the truth where the network’s posterior is, not at its summit alone, and a chain fewer on the one network this was measured on (docs/audit_optima.md).

The posterior pilot (config.posterior, with peaks): after the search for maxima, config.posterior of the cooled T = 1 walkers of the network kept are evaluated. When the network’s mean excess over the truth there is above config.posterior_tolerance, it is fine-tuned on them and the check is repeated with a new chain, at most config.posterior_rounds times; after a last fine-tune the new chain’s summits are reported. Its points are trained on like the others. Without it the audit is as it was.

Parameters:

  • inputs – the arrays the network was just trained on, in the order Keras saw them: its validation set is their last validation_split fraction and stays held out here. The audit points are all trained on.

  • evaluate – true evaluations; a value that is not finite marks a point where the likelihood failed.

  • build_loss – the training loss for a training set with these targets (its best value and the msre scale move when an evaluation beats the best).

  • config – an AuditConfig.

  • n_append – the acquisition’s batch size; with config.k 0 it sets the evaluations per round, config.fraction * n_append / config.rounds.

The returned arrays are the finite evaluations, to be added to the training set. A flagged maximum whose evaluation is not finite cannot become a training point (the run drops such points everywhere); it is set aside for the rest of this audit so the search does not return to it, and the report counts it. With the optional penalize policy, bounded failed probes can justify a separately stored synthetic constraint; it participates in fine-tuning only, never in the returned true-data arrays.

training.audit.posterior_pilot(model, config, look, seen, evaluate, add, current, tune, fit, lower, upper, rng, log)#

The posterior pilot of audit_optima (acquisition.audit.posterior). add(x, y, derived) puts finite evaluations among the audit’s points; current() is (inputs, targets) with them. Returns a dict: the rounds, whether the network was fine-tuned, its summits’ highest excess after the last fine-tune, and, for the caller, the calls made.

audit_it_N.csv (the points added: the run’s parameters, then loglkl, as a training data file) and audit_it_N.json (the report). The CSV is written also when it is empty: a continued run reads it to know what the saved network N was trained on.

training.audit.load_audit(directory, iteration, names, derived_names=())#

Read audit points and row-aligned derived values; old audits leave NaNs.

Newly requested derived columns are also unknown. A present but misaligned sidecar is rejected rather than silently assigning another point’s values.
